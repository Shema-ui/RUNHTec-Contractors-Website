Solar vs generator costs in Rwanda

What a kilowatt-hour really costs from a diesel generator, the grid and a solar system, and when a hybrid makes sense.

By the RUNHTec engineering team · 26 October 2026

A generator is cheap to buy and expensive to run. Solar is the opposite. To compare them fairly, look at what each kilowatt-hour (kWh) really costs you over the life of the system.

What a kWh from a diesel generator costs

A diesel generator running at a sensible load uses roughly 0.25–0.3 litres per kWh. At light loads it is less efficient, often 0.4 litres per kWh or more.

The Rwanda Utilities Regulatory Authority (RURA) set the diesel pump price ceiling at FRw 2,927 per litre in June 2026. That gives:

Generator loadingFuel useFuel cost per kWh
Well loaded (around 70–80%)≈ 0.28 L/kWh≈ FRw 820
Lightly loaded (around 25%)≈ 0.40 L/kWh≈ FRw 1,170

That is fuel only. Oil and filter changes, repairs and the cost of buying the generator come on top.

What a kWh from the grid costs

Under the tariffs approved in September 2025, non-residential customers pay about FRw 355–366 per kWh, and households using more than 50 kWh a month pay FRw 369 per kWh for that extra use. Even at its most efficient, generator fuel alone costs more than twice as much as grid power.

What a kWh from solar costs

Solar has no fuel cost. What you pay for is the equipment and installation up front, then occasional maintenance. Rwanda gets good sunshine, around 4.5–5 peak sun hours a day on average, so each kilowatt of panels (1 kWp) produces roughly:

  • about 4 kWh per day after real-world losses
  • about 1,400–1,500 kWh per year
  • about 30,000 kWh over 20 years, a conservative panel life

To find solar's cost per kWh, divide the installed price by the energy it will produce:

Solar cost per kWh = (installed cost + lifetime maintenance) ÷ lifetime kWh produced

Panels on their own usually work out far cheaper per kWh than generator fuel. Batteries add significant cost, so energy stored for the evening costs more than energy used directly while the sun shines. Because equipment prices change often, ask us for a current quotation for your building.

Why hybrid usually wins

For most homes, hotels and businesses the best answer isn't one or the other. A hybrid system lets solar power the building during the day, batteries cover the evening and short outages, and the grid or generator only steps in when needed. The generator runs far fewer hours, so the fuel bill drops and the generator lasts longer.
